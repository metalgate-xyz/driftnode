# Weighted Follow Graph: Sync Priority Plan

## Problem

`syncAllZens` (daemon.go) reads `FollowGraph()` as a flat, unweighted list
and dials every followed identity with a uniform fan-out (default 8) and a
30s per-dial timeout. There is no priority: a high-latency or offline follow
holds a concurrency slot for up to 30s, and follows the user cares about get
no preferential treatment over the long tail. The result is latency, not
liveness (every follow syncs once per 60s round), but for a social feed,
latency of fresh posts from important follows is the user-visible quality
metric.

The follow graph must become a weighted graph where the weight IS the sync
priority, computed from interaction metrics.

## Metric set

| Metric | Type | State | Computable from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outbound likes | interaction | done | my PostLog `Like.target_id` -> author |
| Outbound replies | interaction | done | my PostLog `Post.parent_id` -> author |
| Pins | explicit | missing entirely | new local state |
| Reciprocal follow | graph signal | available now | crawled profile logs |

Trivial graph signals ("I follow them", "when I followed them") are noise
once you're in the graph and are excluded.

## Scope decisions

- Replies are NOT worth building as a feature: the threaded conversation view
  needs a global index of all replies to a post, which doesn't exist without a
  server (the crawler fetches only Profile logs by design; nobody can write
  into someone else's log; the DHT is for routing records, not one-to-many
  content indexing). But reply as an interaction metric stays: "I replied to
  author X's post" is resolvable from my own PostLog via `Post.parent_id`.
- Cold-start (no interaction history) is a false problem. Losing the db is
  fatal by design with no recovery path (design doc §8), so the only regime
  is "you have your db, hence your interaction history."
- Pins are local-only state: never synced, never signed. Likes and replies
  are signed events in the user's own PostLog.
- The weight formula is designed ONLY after all metrics exist, so
  normalization and default coefficients are informed by real metric
  distributions, not chosen in a vacuum.

## Weight formula (form settled, specifics deferred to phase 4)

Weighted sum of normalized metrics with source-code constant coefficients:

    weight(author) = w_likes   * norm( outboundLikes(author) )
                  + w_replies  * norm( outboundReplies(author) )
                  + w_recip    * reciprocal(author)
                  + w_pin      * pin(author)

The coefficients are tuned constants in source, not user-facing config.
Normalization scheme (log-scaling, saturating soft cap, recency-decay) is
decided in phase 4 against real metric distributions.

## Phase 4 kickoff: metric distribution analysis

The interaction metrics were measured against a synthetic 10k-zen store
generated by `scripts/gentestdb.go` in power-law mode and analyzed by
`scripts/metricsdist.go`. The distributions and the assumptions behind them
are recorded here so the normalization scheme is grounded in data, not
chosen in a vacuum.

### Sizing assumptions

driftnode targets a personal, decentralized network, not a centralized
platform. The realistic ceiling is thousands of zens. Follow-graph sizing is
bounded by Dunbar's number (~150 stable relationships), and observed
out-degree on Twitter (median 82, mean 294 among active users) confirms the
order of magnitude. Interaction follows a participation-inequality pattern
(Nielsen's 90-9-1 rule): most followed authors receive zero or one
interaction; a few receive most of it. The in- and out-degree distributions of
social follow graphs are power-law with exponents between 2 and 3, and per-
post engagement (likes, comments) concentrates on a small set of authors.

### Modeling choices

`gentestdb.go` was extended to generate power-law distributions instead of
the uniform ones it originally produced, because the metrics the weight
formula normalizes must match the shape of real interaction data:

- Posts per zen: Zipf with a bounded tail (mean 100, max 10x mean). Most
  zens post little; a few post a lot.
- Outbound likes per followed author: Zipf (exponent 1.5, bounded by
  -own-like-max). A few authors receive most of the local user's likes;
  the long tail receives zero or one.
- Outbound replies per followed author: Zipf with a steeper exponent
  (+0.8), since replies cost more effort than likes and are more
  concentrated.
- Reciprocal follow: ~30% of followed zens follow back (Mastodon
  migration data showed ~38% edge retention; personal networks skew lower).
- Pins: ~10% of follows, a small explicit set (user-curated, not emergent).

The uniform mode is kept as a baseline only.

### Measured distributions (10k zens, power-law)

| Metric | Authors with signal | Mean | p50 | p90 | p99 | Max | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outbound likes/author | 5700 (57%) | 2.61 | 1 | 6 | 20 | 48 | Power-law, heavy head |
| Outbound replies/author | 2897 (29%) | 2.55 | 1 | 6 | 13 | 15 | Power-law, steeper |
| Reciprocal follow | 2983 (30%) | 1.0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | Binary 0/1 |
| Pin | 985 (10%) | 1.0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | Binary 0/1 |
| Any signal | 8036 (80%) | - | - | - | - | - | 20% cold-start |

### Implications for the weight formula

1. Likes and replies are the discriminating metrics. They span 1-48 and
   1-15 with a clear power-law shape: ~58% of liked authors have just 1
   like, but the top 1% have 20+. This is the signal normalization must
   amplify.
2. Reciprocal follow and pins are binary floor signals (0 or 1). They
   cannot be normalized; they are a fixed additive boost or weight floor.
3. 80% signal coverage means 20% of follows have zero interaction history.
   These cold-start follows need a default weight to stay syncable.
4. The p50 is 1 for both likes and replies, so linear normalization
   collapses the entire bottom half to the same value. Log-scaling
   (log(1+x)) spreads the 1-vs-5-vs-20 distinction that linear scaling
   erases, matching the power-law shape of the data.

### Source material

- Twitter follower graph: median out-degree 82, mean 294, 99% of users
  below 100 neighbors (Microsoft Research, "Quantifying Influence on
  Twitter"). Power-law exponent 2-3 for in/out-degree.
- Twitter follow graph dataset: average degree 3, max 626 across 404K
  nodes (Network Data Repository, soc-twitter-follows).
- Dunbar's number validated on Twitter: max ~100-150 stable active
  relationships (arXiv 1105.5170).
- Mastodon migration: 38% fewer edges than Twitter, lower density and
  average degree (PMC10703911).
- Nielsen 90-9-1 participation inequality: 90% lurkers, 9% occasional, 1%
  creators (NN/G, 2006).
- Nostr empirical analysis: 1.56M active pubkeys, heavy-tailed across
  relays (arXiv 2402.05709).

## Phases

Each metric phase is a vertical slice (feature + its metric end to end),
testable independently.

1. **Replies tracking e2e.** DONE. A reply is a Post with `parent_id` set (not
   a separate event kind or struct). `driftnode post --parent <event-id>`
   creates a reply via the existing Post RPC; the feed prefixes "(reply)".
   `store.OutboundReplies()` computes per-followed-author reply counts by
   resolving `Post.parent_id` to the parent's author across held PostLogs.
   Counts only authors in the current follow graph.
2. **Likes feature and tracking e2e.** DONE. `driftnode like <post-id>`
   command + gRPC RPC + signed Like event into PostLog. Follows' likes
   displayed automatically in the feed (Like events received via synced
   PostLogs). `driftnode feed --mine` filters to own posts. On-demand like
   fetch: `FetchLikes` RPC dials connected zens with a new `MsgLikeRequest`
   sync query, requesting Like events targeting the user's own post IDs
   (best-effort, expensive, user-triggered). Metric: `store.OutboundLikes()`
   computes per-followed-author like counts from own PostLog, counting only
   authors in the current follow graph.
3. **Pins feature and tracking e2e.** DONE. `driftnode zens pin <identity>`
and `driftnode zens unpin <identity>` commands + gRPC `Pin`/`Unpin` RPCs.
Pins are local-only state, never synced or signed. A pin marks a follow edge
for top sync priority: `pinned` lives on the `Identity` proto (follows list),
and pinning requires a follow (`ErrPinRequiresFollow`). The follow graph and
pin flags live in a local identity-keyed cache, kept in sync with the signed
ProfileLog by routing all follow writes through `store.Follow` and all
unfollow writes through `store.Unfollow`; the cache is rebuilt from the log
on backup restore. Pins survive backup restore (in `BackupData`). The
`Pinned` flag surfaces in the follows list (CLI `follows` and the TUI follows
tab). Metric: `store.PinnedIdentities()` returns the pin set for the
`pin(author)` term; the weight floor is applied in phase 5 when per-follow
weights are persisted.
4. **Weight formula.** DONE. Normalization scheme and default coefficients
   chosen against the measured distributions and implemented in
   `internal/weight`. Log-scaling (`log(1+x)`) for likes and replies spreads
   the power-law tail that linear scaling collapses (p50 is 1 for both); a
   saturating soft cap at the measured p99 (likes 20, replies 13) bounds the
   top 1% to a fixed max so the long tail keeps resolution. Reciprocal follow
   and pins are binary floor signals (0/1), not normalizable, applied as a
   fixed additive boost. A pin dominates the sum of all other terms, so a
   pinned follow with zero interaction outranks the most-engaged non-pinned
   follow (the pin is the explicit top-priority mark). The 20% of follows
   with no interaction history get a cold-start default below a single like,
   so any real signal lifts a follow above them while keeping cold follows
   syncable. Coefficients (`wLikes`, `wReplies`, `wRecip`, `wPin`) and the
   caps and cold-start floor are source-code constants in `weight.go`, not
   user-facing config. The package exposes `Weights(Inputs)` (per-follow
   weight map) and `Sorted(Inputs)` (follows ordered highest-weight first,
   identity-string tiebreak for stable scheduler ordering) for phase 5 to
   persist and phase 6 to consume.
5. **Make the graph weighted.** DONE. Per-follow sync-priority weights
   are recomputed and persisted each sync round. `store.RecomputeWeights()`
   gathers all four interaction metrics in a single read transaction (the
   follow set, outbound like/reply counts per followed author, the
   reciprocal set, and the pin set, mirroring the phase-4 analysis script's
   single-pass pattern) and writes each followed identity's weight into a
   new `weight` bucket. `store.WeightedGraph()` reads the persisted weights
   and `store.Weight(id)` reads one. The daemon's `syncAllZens` calls
   `RecomputeWeights()` once per round before dialing, so the weighted graph
   is fresh. Sync is still uniform fan-out; the persisted weights are
   consumed for priority ordering in phase 6.
6. **Scheduler redesign.** Replace `syncAllZens`'s flat `FollowGraph()`
   iteration + uniform fan-out with a priority queue over the weighted
   graph: high-weight follows sync first and every round; low-weight
   follows round-robin across rounds; a dialing peer that times out stops
   blocking the next high-weight follow. Regression test: top-K high-weight
   follows sync in round 1 even when the long tail is unreachable.

## Current state of interaction primitives

`KindLike` is now fully implemented: CLI command, gRPC RPC, signed event
creation, and metric extraction. The on-demand like fetch adds a new sync
query (`MsgLikeRequest`) for requesting Like events by target ID from
connected zens.

Replies (Phase 1) are implemented as a Post with `parent_id`:
`KindReply` and the `Reply` struct have been removed. The metric
`store.OutboundReplies()` resolves reply parents to followed authors.

Pins (Phase 3) are local-only state. `driftnode zens pin/unpin` toggle a
follow's pin flag; `pinned` lives on the `Identity` proto (follows list) and
surfaces in the follows list. Pinning requires a follow. The follow graph and
pin flags are cached together in a local identity-keyed store, kept in sync
with the signed ProfileLog by routing all follow writes through
`store.Follow` and all unfollow writes through `store.Unfollow`. The cache is
rebuilt from the log on backup restore. Pins survive backup restore (in
`BackupData`).

Remaining: scheduler redesign (Phase 6).
